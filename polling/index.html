Read the margin labels. Only of 435 districts carry a real numeric margin. The other are inferred from the race rating, not measured by a poll — individual House districts are rarely polled. Estimated rows are drawn faded and tagged rating throughout. See the methodology.

How far ahead is the leader in each race?

One diverging bar per district. Bars extend right for a Democratic advantage and left for a Republican one; length is the margin in percentage points. Faded bars are rating-derived estimates. Sorted closest-first by default, so the genuinely contested seats sit at the top.

Seat projection

Every district sorted by margin, left (most Republican) to right (most Democratic). The running line is the cumulative Democratic seat count; where it crosses 218 is where the majority is decided. Seats inside ±2.0 points are shaded purple — that band is inside normal polling error, so they are counted as undecided rather than awarded to either party.

Distribution of margins

How many seats sit in each competitiveness band. The tall bars at the edges are the safe seats — the shape of this chart is the real story of a House map: a few dozen races decide it and the rest are settled before anyone votes.
