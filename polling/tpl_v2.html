Correction, July 29. An earlier version of this page reported a national average of D +1.18 and a projected Democratic range of 222–244 seats. Those figures were wrong. They came from a source file whose party labels were scrambled — it listed Rep. Robert Aderholt (R-AL) as a Democratic incumbent and rated AL-07, Terri Sewell's safe Democratic seat, as Solid Republican. In all, districts were mislabelled. This page has been rebuilt from Election Center's own ratings snapshot, which splits 212 D-side / 220 R-side and matches the current chamber. The corrected projection points the other way: Democrats now land at seats, short of the 218 needed unless they take nearly every tossup.
Only district has a real public poll. Individual House districts are barely polled — everything else here is a rating-derived estimate, drawn faded and tagged rating. The one genuinely dated polling series on this page is the national generic ballot, under Generic ballot. 3 of 435 seats are unrated (vacant). Candidate names are withheld unless verified — see the district table.
